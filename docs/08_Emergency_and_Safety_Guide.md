## 1. Purpose & Scope

### 1.1 Purpose
This guide establishes official emergency admission protocols, triage classifications, ambulance deployment workflows, clinical safety boundaries, life-safety codes, fire safety procedures, infection control mandates, and hospital security measures at SmartCare Multispeciality Hospital.

### 1.2 Scope
This policy applies 24 hours a day, 365 days a year across the entire SmartCare Hospital campus, emergency trauma ramps, ambulances, resuscitation bays, critical care transport, and disaster management zones.

## 3. Emergency Triage System (The 5-Level Manchester Triage Protocol)

Upon arrival at the emergency bay, patients are evaluated within **60 seconds** by an Emergency Triage Nurse and categorized by clinical acuity:

| Triage Level | Clinical Category | Target Physician Assessment Time | Example Conditions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Level 1 (Red)** | **Resuscitation (Immediate Life Threat)** | **Immediate (0 Minutes)** | Cardiac arrest, respiratory failure, severe polytrauma, massive hemorrhage, ongoing seizure. |
| **Level 2 (Orange)** | **Emergent (High Risk / Severe Pain)** | **Within 10 Minutes** | Acute chest pain (suspected STEMI), acute stroke symptoms within window, severe respiratory distress, acute anaphylaxis. |
| **Level 3 (Yellow)** | **Urgent (Potentially Serious)** | **Within 30 Minutes** | Moderate asthma exacerbation, severe abdominal pain, compound fractures without vascular compromise, high persistent fever in infants. |
| **Level 4 (Green)** | **Less Urgent (Stable Condition)** | **Within 60 Minutes** | Simple fractures, minor head injury without loss of consciousness, mild sprains, minor lacerations requiring sutures. |
| **Level 5 (Blue)** | **Non-Urgent (Sub-Acute / Chronic)** | **Within 120 Minutes** | Minor skin rashes, chronic lower back pain, routine medication refills, mild upper respiratory cold symptoms. |

## 5. Critical Emergency Conditions — Actionable Public Guidance

> **EMERGENCY WARNING:**  
> The following conditions are medical emergencies. Do NOT wait for a scheduled outpatient doctor appointment. Immediately call **+91-40-6999-0000** or proceed to the nearest Emergency Department.

1. **Suspected Heart Attack (Myocardial Infarction):**
   - *Symptoms:* Heavy squeezing, crushing, or burning pain in center of chest; radiation to left arm, neck, jaw, or back; cold sweats; dizziness; nausea.
   - *Action:* Keep patient seated calmly; do not allow exertion; call ambulance immediately.
2. **Suspected Acute Stroke (Use the B.E. F.A.S.T. Protocol):**
   - **B** - Balance loss or sudden dizziness.
   - **E** - Eyes / sudden loss of vision.
   - **F** - Face drooping on one side.
   - **A** - Arm weakness or numbness.
   - **S** - Speech slurred or garbled.
   - **T** - Time to call **+91-40-6999-0000**. Golden window for thrombolysis is within **4.5 hours**.
3. **Severe Respiratory Distress:**
   - Inability to complete a sentence without gasping, blue/grey tint on lips or fingernails, stridor.
4. **Major Bleeding & Trauma:**
   - Apply continuous firm direct pressure with clean cloth. Do not remove penetrating objects.
5. **Loss of Consciousness / Unresponsiveness:**
   - Check breathing; if breathing, place in side recovery position; if not breathing, initiate CPR and call for emergency help.
6. **Severe Allergic Reaction (Anaphylaxis):**
   - Sudden swelling of tongue/throat, widespread hives, wheezing, low blood pressure.
7. **Accidental Poisoning or Chemical Ingestion:**
   - Do NOT induce vomiting. Bring the chemical/medication container to the emergency room.

## 7. Hospital Safety, Infection Control & Security Policies

### 7.1 Patient Safety Protocols
- **Fall Prevention:** Bedside rails must be kept elevated for all post-operative patients, elderly individuals, and patients receiving sedatives.
- **Medication Safety:** Every drug administered is double-checked using the hospital "Five Rights" rule (Right Patient, Right Drug, Right Dose, Right Route, Right Time).
- **Two-Identifier Rule:** Clinical staff verify patient identity using Full Name and UHID barcode prior to blood draws, medication administration, and diagnostic scans.

### 7.2 Infection Control Mandates
- **Hand Hygiene:** Alcohol-based hand rubs are mounted at every room entrance. Patients and visitors must sanitize hands upon entering and leaving rooms.
- **Masking Guidelines:** Face masks are mandatory in intensive care units, oncology chemotherapy wards, and the emergency department.
- **Isolation Wards:** Patients with airborne or droplet communicable infections (e.g., open pulmonary TB, influenza) are accommodated in negative-pressure isolation suites.

### 7.3 Fire Safety & Campus Evacuation
- The hospital is protected with automated smoke detectors, water sprinkler grids, and pressurized emergency stairwells.
- In the event of a fire emergency:
  - Do **NOT** use elevators.
  - Follow the green illuminated **Emergency Exit** signage to the nearest stairwell.
  - Gather at the designated primary **Emergency Assembly Point: Open Lawn adjacent to Gate 3**.
  - Follow instructions given by designated Floor Fire Wardens wearing high-visibility orange vests.

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**Q1: Do I need to book an appointment before visiting the Emergency Department?**  
A: Absolutely not. The Emergency Department operates 24/7 without appointments. Any patient with an acute medical concern can arrive directly at Gate 1.

**Q2: What is the emergency phone number to call for an ambulance?**  
A: Call **+91-40-6999-0000** for immediate emergency ambulance dispatch and trauma guidance across Hyderabad.

**Q3: How are patients prioritized in the Emergency Department?**  
A: Patients are triaged according to clinical urgency, not arrival order. Life-threatening conditions (Level 1 Red) are attended to immediately, while stable minor issues (Level 4 Green) may experience a wait.

**Q4: What should I do if someone near me collapses and stops breathing?**  
A: Call **+91-40-6999-0000** immediately. Place the patient on a firm flat surface, tilt head back slightly, and begin hands-only CPR: push hard and fast in the center of the chest at 100–120 beats per minute until emergency medical help arrives.

**Q5: What is the "Golden Hour" for acute heart attacks and strokes?**  
A: The Golden Hour refers to the initial 60 minutes following the onset of acute cardiac or stroke symptoms. Receiving emergency thrombolysis or cardiac angioplasty during this critical window drastically minimizes heart and brain damage.

**Q6: Where is the emergency vehicle ramp located?**  
A: The emergency vehicle ramp is located at **Gate 1 on Road No. 12, Banjara Hills campus**, with direct ambulance roll-in access.

**Q7: Can I walk into the emergency department for a routine checkup or prescription refill at night?**  
A: The emergency department is dedicated to acute illness and trauma. While non-urgent patients will not be turned away, non-emergency conditions are categorized as Level 5 Blue and will experience significant waiting times behind critical emergencies. Routine care is best handled in daytime OPD.

**Q8: What equipment is available inside SmartCare ACLS ambulances?**  
A: SmartCare ACLS ambulances are equipped with transport mechanical ventilators, multi-channel ECG and defibrillator units, syringe infusion pumps, emergency resuscitation drugs, oxygen cylinders, and spine immobilization boards.

**Q9: Does insurance cover emergency room treatment?**  
A: Emergency inpatient hospitalizations exceeding 24 hours are covered under standard health insurance cashless policies. Standalone outpatient ER evaluations (without admission) are covered only if your policy includes an OPD/casualty rider.

**Q10: What should I bring with me to the emergency room?**  
A: Bring the patient's Government photo ID, existing health insurance card, any current medication bottles, and previous hospital summaries if quickly accessible. Do not delay travel to search for paperwork.

**Q11: How do doctors treat an acute stroke at SmartCare?**  
A: SmartCare operates a 24/7 Acute Code Stroke protocol. Patients arriving within 4.5 hours of symptom onset undergo rapid emergency CT neuro imaging and, if eligible, receive intravenous clot-busting thrombolytic therapy or catheter thrombectomy.

**Q12: Can family members stay inside the resuscitation bay?**  
A: During active resuscitation, intubation, or defibrillation, family members are asked to remain in the adjacent Emergency Family Waiting Lounge to allow the medical team unobstructed physical access. Regular updates are provided by the doctor.

**Q13: What should I do if someone accidentally swallows a poisonous household chemical?**  
A: Do NOT induce vomiting or administer home remedies like milk or salt water. Call **+91-40-6999-0000** immediately and transport the patient to the emergency room along with the chemical container.

**Q14: What is a Code Blue?**  
A: Code Blue is an internal hospital emergency announcement indicating a medical emergency (cardiac or respiratory arrest). A specialized resuscitation team of doctors, intensive care nurses, and anesthesiologists responds within 2 minutes.

**Q15: What is a Code Red?**  
A: Code Red is an internal hospital emergency alert indicating fire or smoke detection. Designated fire wardens immediately verify the source and execute evacuation protocols.

**Q16: How should I react if I hear a fire alarm while visiting an admitted patient?**  
A: Stay calm. Never use the elevators. Listen to the floor nurse or fire warden, follow the illuminated green emergency exit signs, and proceed down the stairwell to the Assembly Area at Gate 3.

**Q17: Is police reporting mandatory for road traffic accidents?**  
A: As required by Indian law, road traffic accidents, industrial injuries, assault cases, and suspected poisonings are registered as Medico-Legal Cases (MLC). Medical care is initiated immediately without waiting for police clearance.

**Q18: What is the emergency treatment protocol for severe burns?**  
A: Immediately cool the burn area under clean, cool running tap water for 10 to 15 minutes. Do not apply ice, butter, or ointments. Cover gently with a clean, dry cloth and proceed to the emergency room.

**Q19: Does the emergency room have dedicated pediatric emergency doctors?**  
A: Yes. Pediatric emergencies are triaged by the Emergency Medicine team with immediate on-call coverage from our Level-III NICU and PICU pediatric intensivists.

**Q20: What happens if an emergency patient arrives alone and unconscious?**  
A: Emergency stabilization is provided immediately under the doctrine of clinical emergency necessity. Hospital security and Medical Social Work teams work with local authorities to trace and contact next-of-kin.

**Q21: Can I request an emergency blood transfusion at SmartCare?**  
A: Yes. SmartCare operates a licensed 24/7 blood bank with O-negative universal donor emergency blood packs available for immediate life-threatening trauma resuscitation.

**Q22: What should I do if a patient falls in a hospital bathroom?**  
A: Pull the red emergency pull-cord located near the toilet and shower. This instantly triggers a high-priority alert at the floor nursing station for immediate nursing response.

**Q23: Are emergency room doctors certified in advanced trauma care?**  
A: Yes, all SmartCare emergency physicians hold post-graduate credentials in Emergency Medicine and are certified in Advanced Cardiac Life Support (ACLS), Pediatric Advanced Life Support (PALS), and Advanced Trauma Life Support (ATLS).

**Q24: Can I get tetanus toxoid (TT) injection and wound dressing in the emergency room?**  
A: Yes. Minor trauma, lacerations, animal bites, and tetanus immunizations are treated 24/7 in the Emergency Minor Procedure Room.

**Q25: What is the procedure if someone suspects snakebite or scorpion sting?**  
A: Keep the victim calm and immobilized. Do not cut, suck, or apply tight tourniquets. Immediately transport the patient to the SmartCare Emergency Department, where polyvalent anti-snake venom (ASV) and intensive monitoring are available 24/7.
